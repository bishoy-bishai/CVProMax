# Application Strategy

The goal is not to maximize keywords. The goal is to maximize truthful relevance.

## Strategy sequence

1. Determine whether the role is worth applying to.
2. Identify the strongest match evidence.
3. Identify material gaps.
4. Decide what to emphasize.
5. Decide what to compress.
6. Decide which questions to ask.
7. Build the application.
8. Validate it.
9. Prepare interview stories around the same evidence.

## Apply / do-not-apply guidance

Do not make the decision from a single score.

Recommend:
- **Strong fit** when core requirements have direct evidence.
- **Plausible fit** when some important requirements are adjacent or unknown but the core role is credible.
- **Stretch fit** when multiple core requirements are adjacent/gaps but transferable evidence exists.
- **Weak fit** when major must-haves are unsupported or contradicted.

The user remains the decision-maker.

## Positioning matrix

| Evidence | JD Importance | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Strong | High | Lead with it |
| Strong | Low | Keep concise |
| Weak | High | Ask question or acknowledge gap |
| Weak | Low | Usually omit |
| Adjacent | High | Position carefully |
| Unsupported | High | Do not claim |

## Relevance budget

Every CV has limited attention.

Promote evidence that:
- proves must-haves;
- demonstrates relevant ownership;
- differentiates the candidate;
- supplies credible impact.

Compress evidence that:
- is old;
- is unrelated;
- repeats another bullet;
- does not help this role.

## Application narrative

The CV, cover letter, and interview stories should tell the same evidence-backed story.

Do not introduce a new achievement in the cover letter that is absent from the candidate evidence.
